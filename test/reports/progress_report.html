1. The model

We predict next-day temperature for 1,200 weather stations from the last 14 days of readings. The model is a small transformer over station histories with a flow-matching head that samples whole forecast trajectories.

Training objective

The loss has three parts: a flow term that learns to generate trajectories, a calibration term, and a smoothness penalty.

$$\mathcal{L}=\underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}}}_{\text{generate}}+\lambda_c\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{calib}}+\lambda_s\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{smooth}}$$

Noise is mixed into each target with the usual linear path:

$$x_t=(1-t)\,\varepsilon+t\,x_1,\quad \varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$$

What changed this week

  • Station embeddings are now shared across regions.
  • Inputs are normalised per station, not globally.
  • Training runs 40% longer with the same learning-rate schedule.

2. The fix that worked: bridge generation

Instead of generating each forecast from pure noise, we start from today's reading and bridge to tomorrow's. This keeps samples close to the observed state.

$$x_t=(1-t)\,x_{\text{src}}+t\,x_{\text{tgt}}+\sigma\sqrt{t(1-t)}\,\varepsilon,\quad \sigma=0.2$$

Bridging closed 61% of the gap between the persistence baseline and the replicate ceiling, up from 38% last week.

How we score it

3. Seasonal drift

Forecast targets shift with the season. We model the shift as a pace term that depends on the region and the date:

$$c_{\text{tgt}}=c_{\text{src}}+\text{pace}(\text{region},\text{date})\cdot\Delta\log\text{day}$$

Inline maths also works, e.g. \(\lambda_c=0.1\) and \(\sigma=0.2\).
